This release finishes a full sweep of Quill for performance, stability and memory.

### Sending photos and files

- HEIC, HEIF, AVIF, JPEG XL, BMP and TIFF pictures now offer the compressed photo option when you drop or attach them, and WebM offers video. Quill converts them to JPEG for sending. On a system that cannot decode a format, the file goes out as a file.
- You can drop, paste or pick more than 10 files. They go out in groups of ten.
- A dragged GIF gets the same drop zones as other media and stays a GIF.
- A photo edit now finishes in the background. Send waits for it, so the original never goes out by mistake.

### Disk space and the cache

- Quill now keeps its cache to 1 GB and clears files nobody opened for 31 days, the limits Telegram Desktop starts with. This is set once per account, only on accounts that never chose limits. If you pick "No limit" in Settings › Data and Storage, it stays that way.
- The cleanup also covers files in the Downloads list. A downloaded file you have not opened for 31 days, or the oldest ones once the cache passes 1 GB, leave the list and download again when you open them. Files you saved with Save or Save as are copies outside the cache and are never removed.
- The low disk warning now shows in every Quill window: chat windows, calls and voice chats too. It stays until enough space is free.
- Downloads that Quill paused because the disk was low now resume by themselves once space is free, also after Quill restarts or reconnects.
- Freeing space during a call no longer ends the call.
- While a call keeps Quill connected on a full disk, downloads and an update download pause instead of filling the disk.
- Quill warns before a low disk would stop it from opening your account, even with a large message database or several accounts.
- A data folder on a drive that was unplugged now counts as unwritable, so Quill disconnects safely instead of reading the internal disk.
- The full disk notification no longer offers Reply and Mark as read.
- "Show cached files" on the full disk screen says so when the folder cannot open.

### Stability

- On Windows, Quill no longer crashes, freezes or quits silently when GPU setup fails or the GPU is lost, and it no longer leaves blank windows when recovery gives up.
- On Linux, lost GPUs, Wayland input, D-Bus and the tray no longer cause crashes or hangs.
- On a Mac, Quill survives a failed Metal setup. Closing the window no longer leaves a dead app, and notifications arrive while the window is hidden.
- Quitting during a call now hangs up. A Dock Quit or a log out asks before ending a running export.
- Malformed animated stickers are refused before they reach the renderer.
- Text with hundreds of stacked marks no longer slows down the chat or draws over nearby lines.
- If an account cannot open, Quill says why. It reconnects right after a network change, and says when a running copy of Quill is frozen.

### Fewer freezes

- A long multi-line paste no longer freezes Quill on every keystroke.
- Settings saves, photo edits, drag hovers, opening call devices and starting audio no longer hold up the window.
- The window keeps drawing and answering input through a flood of updates, including on big accounts.
- Videos keep playing after their sound ends, and a clip that shows no picture now ends or fails.

### Lighter on the machine

- Chats draw with less work during scrolling and while stickers play, and Quill redraws only what animates.
- A GIF file in a chat no longer holds hundreds of megabytes to show one picture.
- An open chat no longer grows without limit in memory.
- Converting video is about 4 times faster on Linux.

### Also fixed

- Date pickers accept a time in the repeated hour on the night clocks go back.
- Stickers with no thumbnail show their first frame in the panel.
- A song you return to resumes where you stopped, instead of slightly ahead.

### Changed behavior

- On a Mac, closing hides only the main window, and a Dock click brings it back.

### Not yet tested on real hardware

These fixes passed automated tests, but we have not run them on the real thing:

- 120 Hz and HDR displays, and GPU loss on Windows and Linux
- Calls between two real devices
- A real IPv6 router change
- A really full disk with a live connection
- Sending a real HEIC, and decoding HEIC on Windows and Linux
